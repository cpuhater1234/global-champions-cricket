# Build45 panel  -  art direction and composition

Independent subjective assessment; no celebrity/designer credentials implied. Feedback only. Score **6.5/10** for the visible direction. This score is not a whole-game audit or a release gate.

## Evidence and scope

Read compiled `deliver/GCC_v8_Playtest_45.html`, Build44's native-review account and Build42's creative review. Viewed actual Build44 fixture/management/story images and actual Build43 XI image. These are explicitly older browser evidence: they establish the inherited visual direction, not native verification of Build45. The current45 source adds a fixture jump without restructuring fixture order; source review does not prove its browser geometry. Also viewed actual Build45 `qa/panel45/captures/home-desktop.png` and `matchday-desktop.png`. Also viewed actual45 `fixture-jump-mobile.png` and `management-mobile.png`. These verify the focused Round9 fixture destination and Justin Kemp management composition at the captured mobile size. Older XI/story/McCullum images remain explicitly older evidence.

## What earns the score

The charcoal base, strong Punjab red, bounded panels and legible plain bold typography form a coherent management-game shell. It no longer looks like a cream document disguised as a game. The existing GCC crest is a useful identity anchor; the player name/overall pairing and paired fixture scores are readable. The actual44 small-phone story gets its main action into view, and the actual43 XI has a clear red return action. Retain these decisions. The actual45 home uses the existing crest and real Derby photograph well; it feels tangible and coherent. The shortfall is art-directed hierarchy, not a need for a new colour system.

## Five ranked substantive problems

### 1. Too many equally important boxes flatten the composition

**Evidence:** actual44 management has an outer modal, framed order editor, framed relationship section, three grey metric tiles and another outlined commitment area. Actual43 XI stacks header, selection summary, venue panel, two large local tabs, roster heading and player rows. Actual45 matchday reinforces this: a metric strip plus match panel and dressing-room panel all carry red top borders; the match panel subdivides actions, conditions, venue and teams. The same border strength, dark value and generous control weight recur across different levels.

**Impact:** the eye sees containers before the selection problem. It feels like a solid administrative interface, but lacks the controlled focal point expected of a premium sports management game.

**Acceptance:** compare management and XI at390×844 and desktop. A viewer should identify the player/selection issue first and primary decision second within five seconds. Preserve solid bounded outer panels; reduce internal nesting to dividers or spacing where it repeats the outer grouping. No new gradients, decorations or palette changes. A before/after should show fewer competing full outlines without reduced touch targets.

### 2. The next fixture is still compositionally subordinate to the archive

**Evidence:** actual44 Round8 fixture entry opens on Rounds1-3; its current card was measured at y1984.6 in the native review. Compiled45 adds “Jump to Round N” but still maps the schedule chronologically with no summary fixture before history. Actual45 fixture-jump-mobile shows the whole Round9 target visibly bounded by a gold focus ring, with Round8 above and Round10 below. The jump works visually in this capture; this is a subjective criticism of initial information priority, not a remaining reachability defect. Build45's navigation improvement deserves credit.

**Impact:** a season screen first communicates what happened weeks ago. The user must operate a utility control to reach what matters now, weakening momentum and the feeling of managing an active campaign.

**Acceptance:** on a midseason fixture entry at320/390px, the next opponent and round should be readable in the initial viewport, with past results available in chronological order below or in a clearly secondary history group. If retaining jump-only behaviour, test its discoverability with an unfamiliar user. The captured destination already meets the clear-arrival criterion with its gold focus ring; preserve that success.

### 3. Management gives explanatory prose more visual territory than the decision

**Evidence:** actual44 McCullum Management shows “YOUR RELATIONSHIP”, “Know your player”, expectation copy, metric tiles, recent innings, “Make a commitment”, guidance and medical rules before the first promise choice starts at the bottom of the844px image. Actual45 Kemp Management confirms the concern title “Wants a move” appears clearly, but a further nested “Why this matters” panel, three metric tiles and recent innings/spells consume the remainder of the viewport; only the start of “1 full bowling opportunity remaining” reaches the bottom. The action/consequence is still lower than the framing.

**Impact:** this is a strong potential game mechanic presented as a policy panel. The consequential sporting decision loses urgency beneath explanatory framing.

**Acceptance:** at390×844, retain the player identity and make the live concern plus its current promise/progress or at least one concrete available decision visible without scrolling. Put detailed rules in an existing-style disclosure or secondary support block, preserving access and accurate consequences. Test a player with no concern, a move request and an active promise so the hierarchy follows actual state.

### 4. XI control chrome crowds out the team itself

**Evidence:** actual43 XI390 screenshot reaches only one complete player row and the start of the next by844px. Large global/local navigation, summary, venue copy, roster instructions and two action levels precede it. Compiled45 retains per-row arrows/swap and the selection/venue structures. The sticky return action is a real usability improvement, but does not change the low roster yield.

**Impact:** selecting eleven feels like traversing a long form. The lineup should be the central sporting object, yet names receive less initial space than navigation and framing.

**Acceptance:** at390×844 with normal text size, show at least three recognisable player names when the roster section first arrives, while retaining44px hit areas and the sticky completion action. Prefer tightening repeated framing or grouping row operations to reducing name size. Verify position11 controls remain unobscured and discoverable.

### 5. Matchday promotes the venue name above the actual contest

**Evidence:** actual45 `matchday-desktop.png` gives the long “Maharaja Yadavindra Singh International Cricket Stadium” title a three-line, roughly24px treatment, while “vs Gujarat Titans” is a small bold label above. The home/away club panels only begin near the bottom of the666px viewport. The Watch match action is visible, which is good, but its sporting subject is visually weaker than the venue metadata.

**Impact:** the screen's largest text answers “where?” before “who am I playing?”. That misallocates visual drama on the game's central preparation screen. This is a concrete hierarchy issue, not an argument for more stadium art.

**Acceptance:** at1150×666, identify both competitors and the current round before reading the full venue name. Make the contest the main title and treat the existing venue thumbnail/name as supporting information. Preserve the real stadium image and club colour bands; test the longest venue/club names at390px and desktop. Keep the match action in the initial viewport and do not enlarge the photograph.

## Recommendation

Keep this art direction and remove competition within it. The useful next pass is hierarchy, roster density and decision presentation. Do not restart the branding or inflate this bounded review into whole-game premium approval.
