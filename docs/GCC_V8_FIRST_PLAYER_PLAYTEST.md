# First-player observation protocol

This protocol is ready to run; no unfamiliar-player sessions have been conducted by the automated audit. Its purpose is to find misunderstanding, friction and boredom that scripted tests cannot establish. See `GCC_V8_EXPERIENCE_TEST_PROGRAMME.md` for the parallel AI/technical lanes and `qa/experience-programme/human-session-record.json` for a blank session record.

## People and conditions

Recruit a small mix of cricket followers and management-game players who have not seen GCC. Record their cricket familiarity, management-game familiarity, browser, display scaling and device. Observe desktop and actual touch-device play. Counterbalance which device comes first so familiarity does not masquerade as better mobile usability. A small qualitative sample finds problems; it does not estimate population satisfaction.

Use the current playable build and a fresh browser save. Obtain consent before recording. Keep observer assistance and game failures separate. Do not explain control names, ideal XI composition or the purpose of ratings before participants encounter them.

## Tasks and neutral prompts

1. **Start a career.** “Take over a club and build a squad you would be happy to manage.” Observe whether the signing window is mistaken for club recruitment, how candidates are compared, and whether squad needs influence picks. Ask “What are you considering?” without recommending a player or filter.
2. **Prepare the opening match.** “Choose your team and get it ready to play.” Observe XI selection, ordering, bench discovery and lodging. Ask the participant to explain who they expect to open, keep wicket and supply bowling overs. Record unsupported assumptions as well as wrong clicks.
3. **Respond to a result.** After a real defeat, ask “What happened, and what would you consider changing?” Observe whether they find relevant scorebook evidence, distinguish observation from causation, and can reach the relevant selection control. Do not tell them that every loss needs a change.
4. **Prepare season two.** “Build the squad you want for the next season.” Observe whether automatic retention, retirements, optional trading, optional releases, draft-pick costs and final confirmation are understood. Before any confirmed release or trade, ask “What do you expect to happen?” Record expectation versus actual outcome.
5. **Return later.** Resume the same career after a break. Observe whether the participant recognises the current stage and can continue without retraining. A prepared late-season fixture can test navigation, but it cannot establish attachment to a team they did not build.

## Record for each task

- Intended action, actual route, wrong turns and requests for help.
- Time spent searching for the next action, plus total task time. Keep exploration distinct from being stuck.
- What the participant thought a rating, warning or transaction meant; exact wording where possible.
- Important information missed because it was below the fold or inside a disclosure.
- Whether the consequence matched the participant’s prediction.
- Whether small text, crowding, excessive scrolling or repeated steps interrupted a decision.

Ask after each task: “What was unclear?” and “What would you expect to happen next?” Save overall preference questions until after observation to avoid replacing behaviour with politeness.

## Triage

Treat unreadable essential information, lost progress, unanticipated irreversible changes, blocked progression and inability to identify the next action as release blockers. Prioritise misunderstandings repeated independently across participants. Fix the underlying cue or route, then repeat the task with a fresh participant; a trained participant is weak evidence that onboarding improved.

## Sustained enjoyment

Use returning participants across several real sessions for long-term pacing. Ask whether they still have a specific squad-building goal, whether a draft changes their plans, and whether results suggest understandable choices. Record voluntary continuation and abandoned careers with reasons. Completing a simulated nineteen-season run or opening a season-twelve fixture cannot answer these questions.
